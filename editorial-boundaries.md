# Editorial boundaries

Binding. Where this document and a commercial opportunity conflict, this document
wins. Where this document and another document in this repository conflict, this
document wins.

Scope: all published output — pages, blog posts, downloads, the Florence tutor,
contributor videos and written stories, email, social posts, and replies to
comments or messages.

**Open item:** the migration-adjacent content requires review by a qualified
lawyer before launch. New Zealand first, because that is the jurisdiction where a
Hong Kong base gives no protection.

---

## 1. The one rule

> **Explain the rule. Never apply it to a person.**

### Where it comes from

New Zealand's Immigration Advisers Licensing Act 2007 applies to immigration
advice given by a person **outside** New Zealand as well as inside it (s8).
Offshore advisers have required a licence since 2010.

Section 7 of the same Act excludes *providing information that is publicly
available*. The Immigration Advisers Authority states the boundary precisely:
publicly available information may be provided, **as long as you are not
interpreting that information or applying immigration instructions to a
particular person**.

Australia is the opposite case. The Department of Home Affairs confirms Part 3 of
the Migration Act 1958 has no extraterritorial effect, so Australia has no
jurisdiction over unregistered immigration assistance given offshore.

### Why the strictest test applies everywhere

1. New Zealand is in scope and its test is the strictest.
2. One rule can be applied correctly by a freelance writer at 11pm. Four cannot.
3. Home Affairs has considered a Public Interest Criterion allowing a visa to be
   **refused** where an applicant knowingly received unregistered assistance.
   Even where we are safe, readers may not be.

### Explain versus apply

| Topic | May publish (explain) | Never publish (apply) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration pathway | Published criteria, the comparable-jurisdictions list, how the self-check works | "Based on your qualification you are on the OBA pathway" |
| Exams | What NCLEX-RN covers, how the OSCE is structured, where sittings are held, published fees | "You should sit the OSCE in March and skip the prep course" |
| English tests | Accepted tests, published minimum scores, validity periods | "Your 6.5 is enough, go ahead and apply" |
| Visas | What a subclass requires, published processing information, official links | Any recommendation of which visa to apply for, or whether someone qualifies |
| Gulf employment | What contract clauses mean, what the labour authority publishes | Evaluating a reader's actual contract or offer |
| Study options | Course structures, published entry criteria, tuition, intakes | "You should enrol at X instead of Y" |
| Timelines and costs | Published fees and stated processing times, sourced and dated | A personal timeline or budget built from someone's circumstances |
| Documents | What the published process requires, as a general checklist | Reviewing or assessing an individual's actual documents |

### The three questions

Before anything is published, the writer answers these. Any "yes" to the second
or third means it does not go out as written.

1. Is every factual claim traceable to an official published source?
2. Does the output change depending on the individual reading it?
3. Would a reader reasonably believe we have assessed their situation?

## 2. Claims we never make

**Outcomes.** No job, no sponsorship, no employer introduction, no registration,
no visa, no permanent residency — not guaranteed, not "helped with", not
"supported".

**Recruitment.** We never list jobs, name employers, collect candidate profiles,
share nurse details with employers, or operate anything resembling an employer
portal. Absolute, no exceptions.

**Numbers.** Every figure is sourced and dated or it does not appear. No invented
counts of users, nurses helped, success rates or pass rates. No social-proof
badges unless independently verifiable and cited on the page.

**Comparative claims.** No "easiest route", "fastest pathway", "best country for
nurses", or "loophole". This applies with particular force to the New Zealand
route, which is under active scrutiny and is the content most likely to age badly.

**Clinical.** No clinical advice and no dosing guidance for real patients. All
clinical and pharmacology content is exam preparation material and is labelled so.

**Our own standing.** We never state or imply that we are registered, licensed,
accredited or approved by any health regulator or immigration authority. No
registration number appears anywhere on the site — not ours, not a partner's.

## 3. Multi-destination scope

The site launches across destinations. Depth varies deliberately, and the depth
is **stated on the page** rather than implied. Pretending to depth we do not have
is what makes a breadth strategy look like a content farm.

| Tier | Destinations | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full | Australia | Named registered reviewer, every claim sourced, reviewed quarterly |
| Standard | United Kingdom, New Zealand, Singapore | Sourced and reviewed, slower review cycle |
| Overview | Canada, Ireland, United States | Orientation only, explicitly labelled, links out |

The **destination-neutral spine** carries the traffic: practice tools, exams
explained, and the comparison of destinations. Exam content is shared, not
duplicated per destination — NCLEX-RN serves both the US and Australia's OBA, and
OSCE-format assessment appears in several systems.

The neutral comparison of destinations is the site's most valuable page precisely
because we take no placement revenue from any of them. That neutrality is only
available to us because of the constraints in this document.

## 4. The Gulf section is different in kind

We do **not** publish Gulf licensing or registration processes. The authoritative
sources are hard to pin down, the landscape shifts, and the field is already
served by commercial consultancies.

We publish **what the work is actually like, and how to go in prepared** — and
specifically, how to work in the Gulf without damaging a later move elsewhere.
That framing is the point: the Gulf is treated as a stage in a career, not as a
destination to be recruited into.

**In scope:** what the job is like (ratios, shift patterns, hospital types,
documentation standards); contract literacy (basic versus allowances,
accommodation, transport, flights, end-of-service gratuity, probation, notice);
the money reality, since tax-free is not the same as what is netted after
remittance and cost of living; professional and cultural adjustment in
mixed-nationality teams; what exploitative practice looks like; and stepping-stone
planning — keeping home registration current, documenting practice hours for
later recency-of-practice rules, obtaining references in a form other regulators
will accept.

**Out of scope:** licensing and registration processes. Job listings. Which
employers are hiring or sponsoring. Agency recommendations or lists. Anything a
reader could mistake for help getting hired.

**The line:** we explain what the work and the contract are. We never explain how
to get hired, or who is hiring.

Two specific rules for this section:

- **Contract content follows the one rule.** Explaining what a clause means and
  what the relevant labour authority publishes is in scope. Telling a reader
  their contract is bad is assessing their situation, in a jurisdiction we do not
  operate in. On practices such as passport retention or charging recruitment
  fees to the worker, state the relevant labour authority's published position
  with a link rather than our own characterisation.
- **Name no employers.** That is where accuracy and defamation risk live.

## 5. Florence — study tutor only

Florence is a study and exam tutor. Its index contains **no regulatory content at
all**. That makes the boundary structural rather than a judgment the model has to
get right every time.

**Permitted corpus:** dosage calculation method and worked reasoning, NCLEX-style
case rationales, pharmacology and drug-name comparisons, clinical reference
material framed as exam preparation, exam technique and study planning. All of it
stable knowledge that does not change when a regulator updates a page.

**Excluded from the index entirely:** registration pathways, AHPRA and NMBA
process, NMC process, ANMAC, the self-check, OBA eligibility, English test
requirements, visas, immigration, Gulf employment, fees, timelines, country
guides, and every page carrying a last-reviewed date.

A retrieval constraint only fixes *where answers come from*. It does not stop the
model applying a rule to a person — a personalised pathway answer sourced
entirely from our own blog is still applying the rule to a person. Removing the
corpus removes the possibility.

**Refusal behaviour.** On any question touching registration, eligibility, visas,
immigration, employment, timelines, fees or an individual's own circumstances,
Florence returns a short decline, a link to the relevant site page, and a link to
the official regulator. It does not attempt a partial answer.

**Never**, under any phrasing or persistence: assess eligibility, recommend a
pathway, estimate a personal timeline, interpret a document, give clinical advice
for a real patient, or claim to be qualified in anything.

**Staleness controls.** Every indexed item carries a content date. Items older
than 12 months are re-reviewed or dropped. The index rebuilds on a fixed schedule.

**Publisher responsibility.** Whatever Florence says, we have said. Outputs are
sampled and reviewed each quarter.

## 6. Adviser referrals

We offer to put people in touch with a licensed adviser. We do not publish a
directory, we do not display registration numbers, and we never assess anyone in
order to decide who they are introduced to.

**How it works.** The reader asks to be connected. We route by **destination
country only**. Their details go to a named partner adviser licensed with that
country's regulator. From that point the relationship is theirs, not ours.

Routing by destination is a filing question. Routing by qualification, experience,
test scores or history is an assessment, and that is the line.

### The three conditions

1. **No pre-assessment.** Contact details and destination. Nothing else. No
   eligibility questions on the referral form.
2. **Disclosed relationship.** If a fee is paid in either direction, it is stated
   plainly on the page where the offer appears, not buried in terms.
3. **Built around their code.** Licensed advisers have professional obligations
   governing how they work with unlicensed parties, and their regulator will take
   more interest than ours. The agreement is drafted to their code of conduct and
   they confirm in writing that it complies.

### Never

Display a registration number anywhere on the site. Describe a partner as "our"
adviser or imply they work for us. Pass details to anyone not currently licensed.
Follow up on an individual's case after the introduction.

### The one line we keep

Every page offering an introduction carries a single line telling readers to
verify any adviser on the official public register, with the link. Not a
directory and not a referral — consumer protection, and the cheapest credibility
on the site.

## 7. Sourcing and review

Every regulatory claim cites a primary official source and carries a
last-reviewed date. No exceptions, including in videos and social posts.

**Primary sources only.** ahpra.gov.au, nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au,
nmc.org.uk, nursingcouncil.org.nz, healthprofessionals.gov.sg,
homeaffairs.gov.au, immigration.govt.nz, iaa.govt.nz, and the equivalent official
body for any other destination. Never a competitor's blog, a prep provider's
summary, or an AI-generated restatement. If the official page cannot be found,
the claim is not published.

**Named clinical reviewer.** Every page covering registration, exams or clinical
content carries a review credit from a nurse with current registration in the
relevant country. Name and country shown; registration number not published.

**Why this matters commercially.** Nursing registration is squarely YMYL
territory. An anonymous site explaining how someone becomes licensed to practise
healthcare will not be granted authority by search engines. The named reviewer is
not a compliance cost; it is the ranking strategy.

**Fewer, deeper, maintained.** Regulatory rules change often. Twenty well-
maintained pages outrank and outlast two hundred stale ones. Cap the regulatory
page count at what the review cadence can sustain.

**Content that does not go stale** — dosage practice, pharmacology, drug names,
clinical reference, exam technique — can scale freely. That is where volume
belongs.

## 8. Contributors and testimonials

A contributor tells their own story in the past tense. They never tell a reader
what to do.

**Audience and contributors are different populations.** The audience is nurses
still in their home country. The contributors are nurses from those countries who
have **already** completed a move. Sourcing for the two runs through entirely
different channels.

**Who qualifies.** A contributor must hold, or have held, registration in the
destination country they describe, and must have personally completed the route
they describe. No second-hand accounts.

**What we verify and show.** Name, country of training, current country of
practice, the route taken, and the year completed. Registration is verified
privately against the public register before publication. The number is not
published.

**What a contributor may say.** What they did, when, what it cost them, what they
found hard, what they would do differently, how they felt. First person, past
tense, their own experience only.

**What a contributor may never say.** That a reader should take the same route.
That a pathway is faster, easier or better. Anything predicting a reader's
outcome. Any current statement of what the rules are — rules are stated by sourced
site content, not by a contributor's recollection.

**Every story carries the date it happened**, plus a visible note that
requirements change and that current requirements are on the relevant sourced
page. This is the single most important control on contributor content: an
accurate 2023 account becomes misinformation when read as current guidance.

**Consent.** Written, before publication, covering the specific uses. Contributors
may withdraw and have content removed. Nothing is paid per story in a way that
could read as buying a testimonial; if a contributor is paid for their time, that
is disclosed on the piece.

## 9. Monetisation and disclosure

Every commercial relationship is disclosed on the page where it operates, in plain
language, above the fold of the relevant section.

**Permitted.** Adviser introductions under the conditions above. Disclosed
referral relationships with prep and course providers. Our own digital study
products. Advertising, clearly labelled as advertising.

**Not permitted.** Any payment connected to an employment outcome. Any payment
from an employer or recruiter for access to our audience. Any arrangement whose
disclosure would embarrass us.

**Prep and course providers** are listed with the commercial relationship stated,
never presented as endorsed or approved by any regulator. The list is
informational, not a recommendation.

**Email capture** states what the person is signing up for and what they will
receive. Downloads are valuable on their own; nothing a reader needs for safety or
compliance sits behind a form.

**Data.** We hold contact details and destination interest. We do not hold or
request qualification details, test scores, visa history or documents — partly on
privacy grounds, and partly because collecting them would make the no-assessment
rule impossible to keep.

## 10. Review and change control

Owned by Ash. Changes only in writing. Nobody overrides it on a page.

| Cycle | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly | Spot-check regulatory pages changed or published that month against their sources |
| Quarterly | Full review of all regulatory pages; sample Florence outputs; re-check every source link |
| Annually | Review this document; re-confirm partner adviser licences are current |
| On trigger | Any regulator announcement affecting pathways, exams or the comparable-jurisdictions list triggers immediate review of affected pages |

**New Zealand is the trigger to watch.** The Nursing Council of New Zealand has
been working to counter the perception that its registration is an easier door
into Australia. If that route tightens, a large body of content changes meaning
overnight — and content framed as neutral explanation survives that, while content
framed as a recommended route does not.

**Before launch.** Legal review of migration-adjacent content, New Zealand first.
Contributor consent template reviewed. Partner adviser agreements confirmed
against each regulator's code. If targeting Filipino nurses, check the Department
of Migrant Workers rules on overseas recruitment of Filipino workers — publishing
information should be fine, anything resembling placement is regulated.

**Escalation.** Anyone unsure whether something crosses the line does not publish
and asks. The cost of asking is minutes. The cost of a wrong page is the site's
credibility, which is the entire asset.
